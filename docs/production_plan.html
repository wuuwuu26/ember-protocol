0One-Page Overview

FUN HYPOTHESIS · The Foundation of the Design
Every time you strip an enemy's body, you are rewriting your own body — and the path you can walk next.

This sentence welds "combat," "customization" and "exploration" into one causal chain, not three parallel systems. If you can't state it clearly, don't move forward.

Three Design Pillars (The Approval Standard for Technical Decisions)

#PillarDecision question — cut it if it fails
P1Customization changes gameplay, not just numbersDoes this new mech/module bring a new way to move or a new combat solution? Any module that just adds 5% attack power is cut.
P2Dismantling is a costly choiceDoes this salvageable part carry an opportunity cost? (Rescue an ally / keep resources / change route) Salvage without a trade-off is just trash picking.
P3One full arc in 20–40 minutesDoes this mission contain the four beats of preparation, tension, salvage, and customization feedback? If a beat is missing, rework it.

1Engine & Plugin Selection

1.1 Why UE 5.8

UE 5.8 was released in June 2026 and is the last version planned within UE5, after which Epic moves to UE6. For a 42-month project, this is the most stable choice right now:

  • 5.8 is the end of long-term maintenance —— no gap where "the engine stops being supported halfway through the project."
  • Key capabilities are production-ready —— MegaLights (multiple dynamic lights), Mutable (runtime character assembly), Iris, Chaos Cloth, Audio Insights.
  • A UE6 migration window can be reserved —— Schedule a 2-month evaluation period in P4. Don't bet on UE6 now.
Decisions explicitly NOT made

Do not upgrade the engine major version mid-development. Engine upgrades happen only in the P0 and P4 windows.

1.3 Three Explicit Technical Trade-offs

Trade-off 1: Don't use Mover, but keep the interface

Mover is still officially described in 5.8 as"evolving from experimental toward a stable architecture," not marked production-ready. Approach: define the IRobotLocomotion interface, with CMC as the implementation; climbing, magnetic grip and dashing all go through the interface. Future migration only swaps the implementation layer.

Trade-off 2: Part dismantling uses "fake dismantling"

Chaos per-piece shattering has unpredictable performance with dozens of enemies on screen, and fragments can't reliably serve as pickups. Approach: hide the bone + replace with a pre-made part model. Chaos is reserved for scripted set-pieces in the colossus level.

Trade-off 3: Independent mechs, modules on sockets

Do not pursue "free assembly of any parts" — that is an abyss of content cost. Make one complete character for each of the 3 mech classes; 40 modules attach via bone sockets. Only introduce Mutable when the skeleton proportions need to change.

Why These Three Matter

They all point to the same thing: spend the cost where players can feel it. A new engine feature doesn't mean it should be used; stability and controllability matter more than being cutting-edge.

3Core System Implementation

3.1 Mech & Module Assembly

Target experience: the player drags a part in the customization UI, immediately sees the mech's form change in the preview, and reads live feedback on the three constraints of weight/power/cooling.

Three Constraints — Where Pillar P1 Lands

ConstraintEffectDesign intent
MassAffects movement speed, jump height, hit displacementmake "heavy build" genuinely cumbersome, not pure benefit
PowerAll active modules share a capForces trade-offs: can't fill up on high-drain modules at once

Key judgment

The three constraints check each other — the core mechanism preventing "one best build wins all." Without these three, 40 modules are just 40 number stickers.

3.2 Part Dismantling & Damage Routing

Target experience: the player aims at an enemy's legs rather than the torso, and after shooting them off the enemy genuinely limps instead of just losing more HP.

Hit detection
  └─ Hit bone name (FName BoneName)
       └─ UPartMapData (DataAsset): BoneName → FGameplayTag
            └─ UPartDamageComponent: TMap<FGameplayTag, FPartHealth>
                 └─ Part HP reaches zero
                      └─ Presentation layer: HideBoneByName() + spawn a drop Actor

Why part HP is not put into the GAS AttributeSet: part counts are dynamic (different enemies have different part counts), while AttributeSets suit fixed attributes. A standalone component manages them; GAS only delivers damage via GameplayEffects.

How "Fake Dismantling" Works

  1. One body per part in the Physics Asset, naming convention part_upperarm_R.
  2. When a part is destroyed, HideBoneByName hides that part's bone.
  3. Simultaneously spawn a pre-made part model(physics-simulated), which becomes pickable after landing.
  4. Don't use Chaos per-piece shattering — fragments are uncontrollable and can't reliably serve as pickups.
Where Pillar P2 Lands

Named enemies additionally get a UCompanionCandidateComponent. When part HP hits zero but the core survives, they enter a "repairable" state, and the player chooses repair (consume resources, gain a companion) or dismantle (gain a rare core).

Edge Cases That Must Be Handled

  • All parts destroyed but core alive → enemy enters a "disabled" state rather than dead, still interactable.
  • Player's own part destroyed → temporarily lose the corresponding ability (if an arm is destroyed, that arm's weapon can't be used), auto-repaired at mission end.
  • Multiple hits on the same part → damage doesn't spill to other parts, preventing "shooting the leg equals shooting the whole body."

3.3 Power & Heat (GAS Implementation)

AttributeEffectInitial value
PowerCurrent power, consumed by abilitiesPLACEHOLDER

HeatCurrent heat, only rises, never falls on its own0
HeatDissipationCooling rate, determined by chassis/auxiliary modulesPLACEHOLDER

Heat is a "reverse resource" — the core lever of combat rhythm: attacks, dashes and overloads all add Heat; Heat only drops through cooling actions; reaching the threshold triggers GameplayTag.State.Overheated, locking weapons for 2 seconds PLACEHOLDER.

Validation path

Paper simulation (Excel computes the "sustained output time" for 3 module combos) → first playable prototype records 10 real matches → observe whether players actively manage heat. If players never look at the heat bar, the lever has failed and needs redrawing.

3.6 Salvage & Economy System

Sources · Where materials come from

Dismantling enemies (main) / area wreckage & chests / mission rewards / base recycling station (passive output)

Sinks · Where materials go

Craft modules / craft mech frames / repair mechs & parts / upgrade module tiers / base construction (companion facilities)

Inflation risk

Dismantling is an unlimited source; players can farm the same area for infinite materials, and module prices quickly lose meaning.

Countermeasures (all three at once)

  1. Modules are unlocked with "a blueprint + a rare core"; materials are only a consumable. Rare cores come only from named enemies and key missions, limited in number and not farmable.
  2. Area material decay: repeatedly clearing the same area yields diminishing materials PLACEHOLDER.
  3. Base construction consumes large amounts of materials, acting as a long-term sink that absorbs inflation.

Validation path：Use Monte Carlo to simulate the material-stock curves of 3 player types (speedrunner / collector / grinder) over 30 hours. If the grinder's stock tends to infinity after 10 hours, the sinks are insufficient.

3.8 Save System

A Real Design Tension

PC single-player players expect "save anytime" by default. But in this game, saving anytime means serializing: mech assembly state, ownership of 40 modules, drop positions, enemy part HP, companion relationships, base construction progress — a huge state space, and bug-prone.

Decision：checkpoint-style autosave + manual save slots, no save-anytime quicksave. Each mission has 2–3 checkpoints, matching the 20–40 minute run pace; players can manually save to multiple slots at the main base; save points align with mission beats (after preparation, during tension, before salvage).

Version migration：USaveGame subclass carries a SaveVersion field; write a migration function every time the save structure changes. From P2 on, every iteration must test reading old-version saves. Without this, corrupted player saves after launch would be the most fatal source of bad reviews.

10First 14 Days Execution Checklist

The goal isn't to make a game, but to get the first signal within two weeks on "whether this core loop holds up".

D1—D2
Install UE 5.8; set up a Perforce or Unreal Revision Control repo; establish project directories and naming-convention docs
Output: a committable empty project
D3—D4
Set up C++ module skeleton (RobotCore / RobotCombat / RobotAssembly); integrate Enhanced Input; build a capsule that can run and jump
Output: a controllable gray-box character
D5—D6
Integrate GAS; implement Power / Heat attributes; make a dash ability that consumes power and generates heat; overheat lock
Output: movement where the "burst–pause" rhythm can be felt
D7—D8
Implement UPartDamageComponent; make a bone-name → part-tag mapping DataAsset; make a dummy enemy that limps when shot in the leg
Output: part damage is verifiable
D9—D10
Part destruction → hide bone + spawn a part drop → pickup → material count
Output: salvage loop working end-to-end
D11—D12
Build the ugliest but usable customization UI: 3 slots, changing gear genuinely changes move speed and attack style
Output: customization affects gameplay
D13—D14
Self-test 10 runs, record three data points: do players customize proactively? Do they perceive a difference after customizing? Do players watch the heat bar?
Output: a self-test report + next-step decision
D14 Decision Rules
  • All three data points positive → enter P1, expand module count and levels.
  • Customization imperceptible → stop and redraw the customization system, don't keep adding content.
  • Players don't watch the heat bar → heat lever failed, simplify or replace the mechanic.
The Real Value of This Checklist

Not output, but turning the vague question "is this game fun?" into a concrete experiment with an answer obtainable within two weeks.
